# The YC Blueprint (56 Press)

## Summary

*How To Get Into YCombinator: A Data-Driven Guide Based on Analysis of 533 Records and 269,319 Words* (56 Press, San Francisco, Jan 2025). A single ~280,056-character book assembled from public data — YouTube, Reddit, Hacker News, YC's own material, founder interviews, application examples, and S25 Demo Day data — that reverse-engineers patterns in YC acceptance. AI-assisted authorship; **not affiliated with or endorsed by Y Combinator.** Analyzed through eight grounded NotebookLM `ask` queries; the notebook holds exactly one source.

## Key Extractions

- Created the concept page [[yc-acceptance-framework]], distilling the book's frameworks: the four selection axes, the 1-3 Application Scoring System, the four-phase Problem-Solution Fit / Customer Development validation loop, the Five-Section application framework, the interview mechanics, the Five Fatal Application Mistakes, and the Five Core Elements mindset model.
- Dataset profile: 533 records / 269,319 words / 12 source types / 280,056 characters / 1 source.
- Metric hierarchy for traction claims: Profit > Revenue > Users > Interactions.
- Validation target: "dozens" of customer interviews (commonly 20-30, up to ~70) before applying.

## Notable Claims

Track these for contradiction against future sources or official YC material:

- **"Validation before application wins acceptance"** — the book's core thesis; presented as an inferred pattern, not YC policy.
- **The 1-3 Application Scoring System** is attributed to one alumni team (YC S21), not to YC itself.
- **S25 Demo Day descriptive stats** (e.g. ~153 companies, ~74.5% B2B, ~70.6% AI/ML, ~92.8% pre-seed, ~2.8 avg founders, ~89% with >=1 technical founder) are from public observation; some softer percentages (e.g. "89% validated-problem-first") are **author-coded** and should be treated as interpretive, not measured.
- **Survivorship bias**: the guide studies accepted and visible founders, so its patterns describe winners, not the full applicant distribution.
